## 1. The Mathematics of Recursive Model Collapse

When an artificial intelligence model is trained on the output of previous generations of AI models, a well-documented mathematical failure mode occurs: **Model Collapse**.

Formally, consider an empirical data distribution $\mathcal{D}_0$. In generation $t+1$, an LLM is trained on synthetic text $\mathcal{D}_t$ sampled from generation $t$:

$$\mathcal{D}_{t+1} \sim \mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}_t)$$

As $t \to \infty$, two degenerative phenomena occur:
1. **Early Model Collapse**: The probability density of low-frequency events (the long tail of rare facts, nuanced counter-examples, and investigative citations) approaches zero:
   $$\lim_{t \to \infty} P_{\mathcal{D}_t}(x_{\text{rare}}) = 0$$
2. **Late Model Collapse**: The variance of the output collapses into degenerate modes, where the model outputs repetitive, high-confidence hallucinations that bear no resemblance to original reality.

In a Dead Internet world where autonomous crawlers scrape synthetic articles generated by other LLMs, the web's collective information space is actively undergoing early-stage model collapse.
